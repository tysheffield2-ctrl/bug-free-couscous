# ENCORE 1.0 — Industry Simulation Overhaul

This roadmap is a gameplay contract, not a cosmetic wishlist. The player follows the same industry laws as NPC artists; systems must explain their math and consequences.

## 1. Career Seasons
Player and NPC careers use the same momentum model: breakout, rising, hot, peak, stable, cooling, drought, comeback and legacy. Fame provides a floor, never guaranteed charting, deals or commercial success. Repeated weak releases, oversaturation, declining audience response and weak campaigns can cause a drought; strong music and strategy can create a comeback.

## 2. Negotiation 2.0
Every negotiation exposes Stats-screen-style Interest, Patience and Leverage meters. Counters change those meters based on economics, relationship, competing offers and requested clauses. Parties can walk away. Applies to recording, management, artist signing, publishing, distribution, marketing, touring, endorsements and catalog deals.

## 3. Contracts 2.0
Final agreements render as multi-page legal-style documents with plain-language clause explanations, adviser warnings, page review, initials and a final player signature. Executed agreements remain in a Contract Vault. Clauses must drive actual gameplay rather than being decorative text.

## 4. Representation & Team 2.0
Replace passive generic buffs with managers, agents, attorneys, business managers, publicists, A&R/creative staff, tour managers and marketing/social staff. Staff have identities, specialties, reputations, relationships, negotiated compensation and advice. Advice can be wrong. Hiring and firing use presentation sequences and preserve relationship/history consequences.

## 5. Label 2.0
Player-owned labels receive inbound artist demos and proposals for recording, publishing, distribution, marketing, licensing and catalog administration. Executives provide opinions. Dropping, selling or separating from artists uses a roster-review presentation and has lasting relationship/public consequences.

## 6. Label League 2.0
Explain what Label League is, the scoring inputs and eligibility requirements. Always show the player's label: ranked when eligible, or explicitly Unranked with the missing requirement. Include market share, movement, qualifying releases and historical seasons.

## 7. Industry Competition
Labels have relationships from allied to hostile. Support bidding wars, competing offers, artist/executive poaching, catalog battles, release-date competition and public/business rivalries. Artists can use competing label interest as leverage.

## 8. Finance, Tax & Recap 2.0
Remove unexplained percentage-based career overhead. Expenses come from simulated costs. Taxes are location-aware by city/state-or-region/country and expose taxable income, brackets/rates, deductions/reserves and exactly what was deducted. Rename ambiguous buckets such as Label-business receipts to precise owned-label revenue/profit concepts. Weekly recap shows gross income, contractual distributions with percentages and bases, operating expenses, taxable income, tax reserve, net income, cash movement and a per-dollar distribution view.

## 9. Explainability Standard
No unexplained number, percentage, acronym, modifier, eligibility state or deduction. Important values provide a breakdown or info explanation showing what changed them. Audit every major system for clarity/explainability debt.

## 10. Certification 2.0
Keep normal certification progression through Diamond. Add three truly rare global tiers: Onyx, Obsidian and Eternal. Final thresholds must be calibrated from long-career simulations and must be far above ordinary Diamond-scale success; target design space begins around 100M+ and can place Eternal at 500M–1B certified units if simulation data supports it. Rare tiers use a premium package sequence: darkened screen → fancy box → envelope → formal certification letter → plaque reveal. Record rarity/rank in industry history and preserve plaques in a career trophy/museum view.

## 11. Media Ecosystem
Build fictionalized streaming, video and PULSE surfaces that visualize the player's real catalog and public career: artist profiles, popular songs/releases, monthly listeners, appearances, tours, merch, videos, subscribers, engagement, posts, replies and public sentiment. These surfaces consume shared simulation state rather than inventing separate numbers.

## 12. Touring 2.1 — Expanded Venue Ladder
Touring must offer substantially more progression than Club → Theater → Arena → Stadium. Venue availability and financial risk remain demand-driven and city-sensitive.

Target ladder:
- DIY / Bar: 100–250
- Small Club: 500
- Large Club: 1,000–1,500
- Small Theater: 2,500
- Large Theater / Music Hall: 5,000
- Amphitheater: 8,000–12,000
- Small Arena: 10,000–12,000
- Major Arena: 18,000–22,000
- Small Stadium: 35,000–45,000
- Major Stadium: 65,000–80,000
- Mega Stadium / Festival-scale special booking: 90,000–120,000+

Not every city supplies every venue class. Stadium and special-scale bookings should require sufficient demand, reputation or promoter confidence, while the player can still deliberately overreach where allowed and carry the financial consequences. Venue class changes setup, crew, production, security, travel/logistics, ticket inventory, prestige and incident exposure. Hometown/local demand, tour fatigue, pricing, openers and career season remain meaningful.

## 13. Presentation Moments
Major career events should not collapse into generic toasts. Rare certifications, contract execution, management firing, artist dropping/separation, major bidding-war wins/losses and other career-defining moments receive deliberate presentation sequences.

## Delivery principle
Prefer shared engines over compatibility-layer stacking. Career season, negotiation, finance, relationships and world-state engines should feed every presentation layer. Regression tests must cover old saves, sandbox, long careers and player/NPC parity before release.
